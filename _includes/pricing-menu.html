    OneMusic licensing, explained honestly

    Separate from what you pay us

    This is the part most suppliers gloss over, so here it is straight. Your OneMusic licence is a separate fee you owe regardless of who supplies your music - Spotify, Soundtrack, or us. It is not included in the prices above. What changes depending on your supplier is which tier you land on, and for some venue types that's worth real money.

    APRA AMCOS

    Represents the songwriters and composers - the underlying song. The "sheet music" copyright.

    PPCA

    Represents the recording artists and labels - the specific recording you hear. The "recording" copyright.

    OneMusic

    Bundles both into a single licence, so you deal with one organisation instead of two. Every venue where staff or customers can hear music needs one.

    Which scheme applies to you

    Hotels, pubs & bars

    Your fee is set by a tier based on how many TV screens you have (and whether you run a music system). Six tiers, Sapphire down to Bronze.

    TierQualifiesPer year
    Sapphire10+ TV screens, any radios & music systems$3,944.48
    Diamond7-9 TV screens$2,472.66
    Platinum2-6 TV screens$1,412.95
    Gold1 TV screen, single music system$618.16
    Silver1 TV screen, no music system$235.48
    Bronze1 TV screen, 1 radio$153.07

    eJukebox counts as a music system, so a venue running us needs Gold or above. Incl. GST; the GST-exclusive part rises with CPI each 1 September. 50% off if you're open under 150 days a year.

    Registered clubs

    Clubs are charged per member, not by TV count - and Digital Copy/Delivery is already included in that fee.

    Background music for clubsPer member / yr
    Each member up to 5,000$0.82
    Each member over 5,000$0.36
    Minimum annual fee$158.96
    Digital Copy/DeliveryIncluded

    Worked example from OneMusic's own guide: a bowling club with 1,504 members pays $1,233.28/yr for background music - about $103/month - and that figure is the same whether they run Spotify or eJukebox. Live music or DJs where entry is over $40 is charged separately.

    Retail & service

    Here the tier is driven by your music source, so changing supplier genuinely drops the tier. Rates shown for the smallest 1-50 m² area.

    TierApplies toPer year

    A shop on Spotify sits on Gold at $641.94. On a commercial supplier like eJukebox it drops to Silver at $183.41 - a genuine saving of about $458/yr, before you count the product at all.

    The honest read what leaving Spotify actually saves

    Venue typeOn SpotifyOn eJukeboxLicence saving / yr
    Pub / bar Tier by TV count + Digital Copy/Delivery $470.98 Same tier, no Digital Copy/Delivery ~$471
    Retail / shop Gold tier (e.g. $641.94 at 1-50 m²) Silver tier (e.g. $183.41) ~$458
    Registered club Per-member fee (Digital Copy/Delivery included) Same per-member fee $0 - no licence saving
    We'd rather you heard this from us than found out later. For pubs and shops, switching off Spotify is a real, documented OneMusic saving. For a registered club it saves you nothing on the licence - clubs pay per member regardless of supplier, and Digital Copy/Delivery is already bundled in. If you're a club, the reasons to move are compliance and the product, not a discount on your licence. And to be straight about it: eJukebox costs more per month than a consumer Spotify subscription. The licence saving offsets much of that gap for a pub or a shop - it doesn't make us cheaper outright.
    The one thing that isn't a grey area. Consumer Spotify's own terms forbid commercial and public use, so a venue cannot legally run it - no matter what it pays OneMusic. OneMusic says so in writing: “Even with our licence, the use of digital music streaming services by you in your business may be in breach of the terms and conditions of your end user agreement with that service.” A OneMusic licence covers the music rights; it can't override your contract with Spotify.
    Why we're a licensed supplier and Spotify isn't. eJukebox holds the upstream Australian licences a compliant background-music supplier needs: AMCOS (reproduction of the musical works), ARIA (reproduction of the sound recordings, jukebox licence), and APRA + PPCA for public performance and webcasting - plus registration as a jukebox operator under the MIPI/MRA code of conduct. That's what makes a venue playing eJukebox a venue using a commercial background music supplier, which is what drops the Digital Copy/Delivery add-on and the retail tier.
    OneMusic rates on this page are taken from OneMusic Australia's own published agreements and guides and include GST, current as at the 2025-26 licence period. They're indexed to CPI each 1 September. Your exact fee depends on your venue - we'll work it out with you, and we can collect and remit it on your behalf.
