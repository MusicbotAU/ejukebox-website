What does a background music provider do?

A background music provider supplies the music a business plays to its customers, programmed for the room and cleared for public play. It is a different thing from a streaming app. You get channels built for trading hours rather than personal listening: no ads, no talk between songs, no explicit lyrics, and volume levelled so one track never jumps out over the last.

eJukebox is an Australian owned commercial background music supplier. Our players have been running in pubs, clubs, cafés and shops since 2018.

Channels picked by people

Every channel is built and refreshed by Australian music directors, never shuffled by an algorithm. Hits, Smooth, Rock and Country.

Keeps playing if the internet drops

On Core and Complete, the on-site player caches your music, so a full room never goes quiet because the connection did.

Staff steer, customers request

Staff run the room from the eJukebox Admin app. Customers request songs from their phone's browser or the eJukebox Request app.

Can I just play Spotify or Apple Music in my business?

No. Personal Spotify, Apple Music and Amazon Music accounts are for personal listening, and their own terms rule out playing them in a business. A OneMusic licence does not fix that, because the problem is the account, not the licence.

It also costs you more. OneMusic puts a business playing a personal streaming account on its most expensive package. A commercial provider is how you get off it.

Do I still need a OneMusic licence?

Yes. Any Australian business playing recorded music where customers can hear it needs a OneMusic Australia licence, held in your own business name, whichever provider you choose. What the provider changes is what OneMusic charges you.

  • Shops, salons and clinics move off OneMusic's top tier, where personal streaming puts them, to a lower one.
  • Pubs, cafés and restaurants no longer need the Digital Copy and Delivery add-on.
  • Pubs and clubs can have eJukebox collect the OneMusic fee and pay it to OneMusic for them.

OneMusic sets and publishes its own fees - check yours at onemusic.com.au. How music licensing works →
