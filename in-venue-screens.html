 Friday, half past nine

A table of six have had a couple of rounds, the music is right, and someone leans in for a selfie. Nothing unusual there. That happens in your venue a hundred times a night already.

Except this time they scan the code on the table and send it to your big screen.

Twenty seconds later it is up on the wall, with your logo in the corner.

The table erupts. Someone on the next table looks up, sees it, and reaches straight for their phone. Then the group by the bar. Then the couple near the window who were about to call it a night and are now ordering another round, because something is happening and they want to be part of it.

That is the whole idea. Your customers become the entertainment, and the best night your venue has had all month is playing on a loop on your own wall.

Fifteen seconds, and no staff involved

No app, no sign-up, no account. Someone scans a code, picks a photo, and it is on your screen. It works on any phone in the room.

They scan and send

A code on the table, the wall, or the back of the cubicle door opens your venue's upload page. They pick a photo, add a caption if they feel like it, and send.

It gets checked

Every photo is screened automatically before it can appear, so nudity and anything tasteless never reaches your wall. Your staff keep a live feed with a one-tap remove.

Your brand goes on it

Your logo lands on every photo the moment it is accepted. So every picture that ends up in a group chat or on someone's story carries your venue's name with it.

You almost certainly already have the screen

This is not a "buy our display" product. If there is a TV on your wall with a spare HDMI socket, you are ready. We plug in a small player, and your screen has a new input like any other.

The footy stays exactly where it is. Sport runs on the input it always has. When the game finishes, someone picks up the same remote they always use and flicks across to the photo wall. At kickoff, they flick back. No new screen, no rewiring, no arguments about which TV.

It works with essentially any modern television or commercial display, whatever the brand. If you would rather we supplied a screen as well we can, but for most venues it is a spare HDMI port and nothing more.

Got several screens? Run the photo wall on the quiet one in the lounge and leave the sport untouched in the front bar.

And it quietly solves your social media problem

Here is the honest truth about venue social media: almost nobody has the time. Posting consistently means someone remembering to take photos, edit them, write something, and actually post it. On a Saturday night that person does not exist, because they are behind the bar.

So let the room do it for you. Every photo your guests send is already branded, already checked, and already the kind of content that performs, because it is real people having a genuinely good time in your venue rather than a stock photo of a beer.

At upload, guests can add their own handle and hashtags. So when you post, the people in the picture get tagged, they see it, and they share it on to their own followers. Your reach comes from your regulars instead of your ad budget.

Come Sunday morning, the whole night is sitting there sorted and ready. Pick the ones you like and post. The thing you have been meaning to get around to for two years takes about ninety seconds with a coffee.

Why this puts money through the till

They stay longer

People stay where they are enjoying themselves. That is not a marketing line, it is the entire economics of a venue. Every extra twenty minutes is another round.

It runs itself

No staff member has to do a thing. Once the screen is on the wall and the codes are out, it runs all night, every night, without anyone thinking about it.

People talk about it

The pub where you end up on the big screen is a thing people tell their mates about. Reasons to choose you on a Friday are the hardest thing in hospitality to buy.

Done properly, because it has to be

Putting guest photos on a wall in a licensed venue is not something to be casual about, so it is built carefully.

Nothing appears unchecked

Automatic screening runs on every image before it is eligible for the screen, and your team can pull anything down instantly from any phone behind the bar.

Clear permission, up front

Before anything is sent, the guest ticks a plain-English box covering your screens and social use. No small print games.

You set the rules

On for a function, off for a quiet Tuesday. Wide open, or hold everything for approval. Your venue, your call, changeable in a tap.

Interactive Request Screen

The request booth on your wall. Not everyone wants to pull out their phone, so give the room a screen. A sleek, wall-mounted touchscreen lets anyone walk straight up, browse your library and pick their own song in seconds. Think of the self-order screens at McDonald's, but for music.

How it works

The screen mounts on your wall and connects to your player. Customers tap to browse the library and send a request, and it slots straight into the queue and plays, running the very same request engine as the phone experience.

Where it shines

Busy floors where phones come and go, family venues where not everyone has an app open, gaming rooms, bowling clubs, and anywhere you want a fun, hands-on way for guests to shape the music.

Why it lands

It makes your venue feel modern and interactive from the second someone walks in, a genuine point of difference that gets the whole room talking and keeps people drifting back to see what is on the screen.
