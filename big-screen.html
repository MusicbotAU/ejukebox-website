The Whole Room Wants To Be On That Screen

Your guests upload their own photos. Your logo goes on every one. Your venue becomes the story people take home.

Picture a Friday night in your venue

It is about half past nine. A table of six have had a couple of rounds, the music is right, and someone leans in for a selfie. Nothing unusual there, that happens in your venue a hundred times a night already.

Except this time they scan the code on the table and send it to your big screen.

Twenty seconds later it is up on the wall. Your logo sitting neatly in the corner. The table erupts. Someone on the next table looks up, sees it, and reaches straight for their phone. Then the group by the bar. Then the couple near the window who were about to call it a night and are now ordering another round, because something is happening and they want to be part of it.

That is the whole idea. Your customers become the entertainment, and the best night your venue has had all month is playing on a loop on your own wall.

How it actually works

No app. No sign-up. No staff involvement. Someone scans a code, picks a photo, and it is on your screen. That is the entire flow, and it works on any phone in the room.

They scan and send

A code on the table, the wall, or the back of the cubicle door opens your venue's upload page. They choose a photo from tonight, add a caption if they want, and send. It takes about fifteen seconds and nobody has to download anything.

It gets checked

Every photo is scanned automatically before it can appear, so nudity and anything tasteless never reaches your wall. Your staff keep a live feed with a one-tap remove, and you can switch the whole thing to hold-for-approval on the nights that call for it.

Your brand goes on it

Your logo is placed on every photo the moment it is accepted, alongside ours. So every picture that ends up on a camera roll, in a group chat, or on someone's story is carrying your venue's name with it.

The part that solves your social media problem

Here is the honest truth about venue social media: almost nobody has the time. Posting consistently means someone remembering to take photos, remembering to edit them, remembering to write something, and then remembering to actually post it. On a Saturday night that person does not exist, because they are behind the bar.

So let the room do it for you. Every photo your guests send is already branded, already checked, and already the kind of content that performs, because it is real people having a genuinely good time in your venue rather than a stock photo of a beer.

A whole night, bundled

Come Sunday, the entire night is waiting for you in one place, sorted and ready. No hunting through a camera roll, no editing, no wondering what to write. It is already done.

Tags, from the people in the photo

At upload, guests can add their own handle and any hashtags they like. So when you post, the people in the picture get tagged, they see it, and they share it on to their own followers. Your reach comes from your regulars instead of your ad budget.

One tap to publish

Pick the ones you like and post. That is the whole job. The thing you have been meaning to get around to for two years takes about ninety seconds on a Sunday morning with a coffee.

Why this puts money through the till

People stay where they are enjoying themselves. That is not a marketing line, it is the entire economics of a venue. Every extra twenty minutes someone stays is another round, and a room with something happening in it holds people far longer than a room with a television showing the cricket on mute.

It costs you nothing to run. No staff member has to do a thing. Once the screen is on the wall and the codes are out, it runs itself, all night, every night.

And it makes your venue the one people talk about. The pub where you end up on the big screen is a thing people tell their mates about. That is a reason to choose you on a Friday, and reasons to choose you are the hardest thing in hospitality to buy.

Done properly, because it has to be

Putting guest photos on a wall in a licensed venue is not something to be casual about, so it is built carefully.

Nothing appears unchecked

Automatic screening runs on every image before it is eligible for the screen, and your team can pull anything down instantly from any phone behind the bar.

Clear permission, up front

Before anything is sent, the guest ticks a plain-English box confirming they are happy for the photo to appear on your screens, and to be used by your venue and by eJukebox on social media. No small print games.

You set the rules

Turn it on for a function and off for a quiet Tuesday. Run it wide open, or hold everything for approval. It is your venue and your call, changeable in a tap.
