You have years of good photos of her sitting on your phone. The one from the road trip. The one nobody has seen. You scan the code on the table, pick one, and seconds later the whole room is looking at it.

So does her best friend. So does her uncle. So do fifty other people who came to celebrate and would never in a million years have stood up to make a speech.

Nobody organised it. Nobody was handed a microphone. It simply happened, all night, because the room was finally given a way to join in.

That is the difference between a room with music in it and a night people are still talking about years later.

The questions everyone asks first

What stops someone putting something awful on the screen?

Every photo is screened automatically before it can appear, and every voice dedication is transcribed and checked before it airs. On top of that your staff keep a live feed with a one-tap remove. Nothing reaches the wall unseen, and nobody has to stand there watching it all night.

Do guests need to download an app?

No. They scan the code and it opens in their phone's browser. No app, no sign-up, no account, no password. That is deliberate, because anything more and half the room will not bother.

Do we need to buy a screen?

Usually not. A TV or commercial display with a spare HDMI socket is all it takes. If you would rather have a dedicated screen or a wall-mounted touchscreen we can supply one, quoted for your venue, with no margin loaded onto the hardware.

What if the host wants the music left alone?

Then it is. Photos, requests and dedications can each be switched on or off for the night. Some hosts want the whole room running it, others just want the controls and a good channel. Both work.

Does this only work for functions?

No, and that is rather the point. The same system runs every night you are open. The function features simply hand the room over for one evening.
