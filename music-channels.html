How a channel is made

Built like a radio station, not shuffled like a playlist

Chosen by people

Australian music directors

Music directors from commercial radio choose every song and how often it comes round, the way a radio station programs a shift. An algorithm optimises for one listener. A music director has stood in a full bar at nine o'clock on a Friday.

Kept current

Refreshed every fortnight

Every two weeks the channels are refreshed using logged Sydney commercial radio rotations, so what plays in your venue tracks what your customers hear on the radio, and the room never gets stuck on the same fifty songs.

Right for the hour

Dayparting built in

Lighter mornings, upbeat nights and separate weekend modes. The channel can change on its own as the day goes on, so nobody behind the bar has to think about the music.

The sound

It just plays. Nothing jumps, nothing interrupts.

Every track at the same level. Levels are matched track to track in the library itself, and every song runs through broadcast-grade multiband processing, the same class of processing FM radio stations use. A quiet ballad and a loud rock track land at the same level, so staff set the volume once and leave it.

Song to song, like radio. Tracks flow into each other whether they fade out or end cold. No gaps and no dead air.

No ads, no idents, no talk. Nothing plays between songs unless you put it there yourself, like your own announcements.

Clean and licensed

Play it out loud without looking over your shoulder

Clean by default. Every channel is screened for explicit content - no swearing and no explicit lyrics - so the music is as right for a bistro at six as it is for a full bar at eleven.

Licensed for the room. eJukebox is a commercial background music supplier, licensed to supply the same music to many businesses. A personal streaming account is licensed for one person's listening, and its own terms rule out playing it in a business.

Your OneMusic licence is still yours. Every venue needs its own OneMusic licence, whoever supplies the music. Being on a commercial supplier is what changes what OneMusic charges you. How music licensing works →
